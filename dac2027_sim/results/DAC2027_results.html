DAC 2027: compiled results

Our accelerator vs PTB, SpikeX, LoAS, SATO, SpikeFlow, Prosperity (and Phi for storage). Generated from the result files of dac2027_sim; every number below is read from those files.

1. Setup

  • Workload sparse90 (all main results): 90% of int8 weights zero; 90% of neurons silent; a non-silent neuron fires with p = 0.3 per timestep and at least once; T = 8 (about 3.2% of spike bits are 1). VGG16 and ResNet18 (CIFAR shapes).
  • OP = one accumulation per '1' spike bit per output channel (Phi's definition): VGG16 65.4 M OP/image, ResNet18 117.7 M OP/image, the same for every design.
  • Arrays: every design at the size given in its own paper (table below). Common clock 500 MHz (the clock our pipelined core closes at 45 nm); normalized speed-ups do not depend on the clock.
  • Technology: all designs synthesized from our RTL of each design at 45 nm (Nangate45, Yosys + ABC), gate-level power with OpenSTA; SRAM with CACTI 7 at 45 nm (LSTP cells, LOP periphery, 300 K; every buffer meets 500 MHz); DRAM 12.45 pJ/bit (Prosperity repo, from DRAMsim3).

Measured on sparse90, batch 1. Filter packing: filters with a non-zero weight in a K-tile share a column when their non-zeros never fall in the same row (up to F = 4). Pixel packing: pixels whose spike bits never collide in a K-tile share one group (up to G = 4); pixels with no spike in the tile are skipped.

9. Dataflow ablation: WS vs IS vs OS

Same array (4 x 4 PEs, 8 adders per PE), same dual-side packing in all three, same workload (sparse90, batch 1). WS = our design (cycles RTL-verified); IS keeps packed pixel groups stationary and streams packed weight columns; OS gives each PE 4 filters x 4 pixels x 8 accumulators and processes one non-zero weight and one pixel group per cycle per input. IS and OS are analytical models with the same overheads. Energy: same core power for all, SRAM from CACTI per bit, same DRAM traffic.

WS is the fastest (1.3-1.9x vs IS, 3.2-3.5x vs OS) and has the lowest EDP. Its cost is partial-sum traffic to the output buffer (about 90% of its on-chip traffic, 4.5-5x more than OS) and the highest average power, because the same DRAM and SRAM energy is spent in a shorter runtime.

10. Notes and limits

  • Our timing is synthesis-level (no placement or wiring). The baselines' RTL was not timing-closed and is evaluated at the same 500 MHz; LoAS's TPPE does not close it (3.14 ns), so its GOP/s is optimistic.
  • Prosperity's power is measured over a busy steady-state window (16 tiles, processor busy 87%); LoAS on the sparse90 stimulus. SATO's 54 KB buffer is assumed (not given in its paper). PTB has no cross-image batching (official model).
  • Ungated flop clock power is most of every core's power (about 73% of ours); no design was clock-gated.
  • Our DRAM count omits a per-tile column-count header (0.13 MB on VGG16, +1.4%).
  • Not included because they predate the pipelined RTL and were not re-run: the design-space sweep, the bound audit, the weight-density 0.3 / 0.5 runs. Equal-PE (16 PE) comparisons are not included: every design runs at its paper's size.
